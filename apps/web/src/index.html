live

The same register, raised: the machine is the ground (gridded to its CPUs and memory), runs are platforms named on their plates, VMs are blocks whose glow follows serial chatter. f. 7

Ledger

live

The plaza's account-book: every task this node holds, newest first — its folio number in the margin, its goal, the role it was entered for, its budget in credits and the dollars they stand for, and its state as a stamp or a mark. A live agent claims one by its guest CID; the agent that did reports it done or failed, and the owner may withdraw one that has not ended. A finished task's sheet also reads that agent's own work product — the files its note names — through the node, one read at a time. The node keeps no transition history, so a sheet shows the task's present state and invents no journal.

        f. 9
        
    Treasury
    
        live
        
    The plaza's account-book: the accounts this node keeps — the mint that is the source of every credit, one escrow per budgeted task, and the rest of the schema's kinds — each with its derived balance, and the double-entry journal they move through, newest first the way the register reads. Every entry balances to zero and carries its postings as a ruled pair: a task's budget is minted into its escrow when it is entered, and what its escrow still holds is refunded to the mint when the task ends. v0 meters no real spend, so a done task refunds its whole escrow — the node says so in its own words below, shown as it wrote them.

    The accounts 
    
      
    
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    No.KindOwnerBalanceLimit
    
        
      
    
      
    
        
    A balance is derived, not stored: it is the sum of the account's own postings, an entry crediting one account and debiting another. mint is the source of all credits and may go arbitrarily negative — that is its nature; the other kinds are held to their own limit, below which a posting is refused. Every amount is whole credits and 1 credit = $0.001 of real spend, so each figure is shown both ways.
    
      
    

      
    The journal 
    
      
    
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    No.KindTaskMemoAmountPostingsPosted
    
        
      
    
      
    
        
    The journal is append-only and double-entry: every entry balances to zero, and its postings read as a ruled pair — the debit side with a − and the credit side with a + — so a budget minted into a task's escrow reads mint −750 / task_escrow t1 +750. Newest first; the daemon bounds the response to its newest entries, and the count above says what the bound left behind. A task named in a row is a link into its ledger entry.
